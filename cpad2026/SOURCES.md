# CPAD 2026 draft sources

This folder is an uncommitted working draft. The LaTeX source is intended to
make the narrative and quantitative claims reviewable before rebuilding the
talk in Keynote.

## Local material

- `Madrid_FiberCalo_Dec2024_HFCC.pdf`: motivation, HG-DREAM history, readout
  scale, longitudinal timing concept, and the visual red/white TTU language.
- `DigitalSiPMDesignStudy.pdf`: the April/May 2025 occupancy-study sequence,
  original pitch labels, 50 ps time segmentation, one-fiber dead-time model,
  and the historical spatial/time/production-depth plot choices. Its
  sensor-agnostic intent is preserved, while the current talk explicitly
  separates software closure from physical sensor requirements.
- `Madrid_Detector_Challenges_Higgs2025.pdf` and the public DPF talk: current
  Madrid presentation language---white background, black sans-serif titles,
  restrained TTU red rules, direct plot annotations, and little decoration.
- `results/full-detector-v1/`: current full-detector Cherenkov mapping,
  branch-validation, local occupancy, event displays, pitch/recovery response
  scan, and event-level propagation. `plot_manifest.csv` records the question
  and intended claim for every headline plot.
- `results/cpad2026-one-fiber/`: the physically relabelled one-fiber
  segmentation plot and its CSV source table. The histogram contents are the
  exact reproduced 2025 occupancy products; the legend applies the audited
  factor-of-three correction so it reports physical SPAD pitch.
- `results/2025-both/`: exact reproduction of the historical display-scaled
  dSiPM pitch scan. This is software closure, not a physical pitch requirement.
- `results/flash-requirements-2026/`: legacy display-scaled recovery extension.
- `results/flash-study-v2-final/`: current physical-millimeter PDE, pitch,
  recovery, event-tail, pulse-shape, timing-proxy, and input-audit products.
- `results/cpad2026-audience/`: shared-scale x-y photon maps, 50 ps-binned
  photon-arrival profiles, source ROOT histograms, and a JSON summary.
- `results/cpad2026-validation/`: event-level occupancy, optical-transport and
  depth-sliced timing, event-to-event tails, historical closure, source ROOT
  histograms, and the validation summary.

talk, “Digital Silicon Photomultipliers for High Energy Physics.” In
particular, the updated figures preserve its progression from physical photon
density, through channel-size and multiple-hit occupancy, to recovery-time
losses before moving to the full HG-DREAM detector:

“Energy Performance of High-Granularity Dual-REAdout Module with test beam”:

https://indico.cern.ch/event/1572594/contributions/7072387/

- `hgdream_high_granularity_crop.png`: slide 4.
- `hgdream_timing_diagram.png`: slide 5.
- `hgdream_fiber_layout.png` and `hgdream_detector_photo.png`: slide 7.
- `hgdream_em_plots.png`: slide 23.
- `hgdream_pe_plots.png`: slide 24.

The other PNGs are generated by this repository and retain their CSV/ROOT
sources in the corresponding `results/` directories.

The arrival-profile figure is not a measured analog pulse shape. It applies an
idealized non-paralyzable SPAD holdoff to DREAMSim photon timestamps; an
electronics impulse-response convolution is a named follow-up study.

The new `results/pulse-shape-v1/` products close the sensor-only part of that
gap. They apply exponential partial recharge to a representative 3 x 3 mm2
MicroFC-30035-SMT SiPM and use the vendor's 0.6 ns fast-output rise time and
1.5 ns FWHM as a common display kernel. Digital curves are explicitly called
"waveform-equivalent": accepted timestamps are convolved with the same kernel
to aid visual comparison, but are not claimed to be dSiPM voltage outputs.
Every architecture uses the same deterministic 20% PDE selection so the plots
isolate pitch and recovery rather than mixing in uncertain spectral response.

The pulse scan includes 10.17 um Litavis, 61.7 um effective Philips DPC3200,
and 78 um Sherbrooke PDC points. In the reviewed primary material, 78 um is the
largest documented pitch of a fabricated dSiPM-class array. The 100, 150, and
200 um points are labelled simulation stress tests; the 200 um point also
preserves the range used in the capstone work.

- Philips DPC3200 characterization:
  https://cds.cern.ch/record/2255746/files/Liu_2016_J._Inst._11_P01017.pdf

## Device values

The authoritative table for plotted public numbers is `../cpad_benchmarks.json`.
It records the maturity, pitch basis, timing metric, and source URL for every
entry. The deck deliberately does not compare TDC binning, SPTR/IRF, and full
system time resolution as if they were the same observable.

The conventional-SiPM curve is a representative Onsemi MicroFC-30035-SMT
C-Series proxy, using the official data sheet:

https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/microc-series-d.pdf

The model uses 31% PDE at 420 nm and 2.5 V overvoltage, 4,774 data-sheet
microcells, an 82 ns recharge constant, and the reported 1.5 ns fast-output
FWHM. A 69 x 69 square grid (4,761 cells) approximates the microcell map, and
successive avalanches contribute exponential partial-recharge charge. This is
an optimistic sensor-only proxy, not a model of the complete installed
HG-DREAM analog chain.

The event-display slide deliberately separates response topology from that
sensor-only performance proxy.  Its HG-DREAM panel uses the installed
Cherenkov map: 64 core channels grouping three rods onto 3 x 3 mm2 SiPMs and
384 outer channels grouping 12 rods onto 6 x 6 mm2 SiPMs.  The original-DREAM
panel is a geometric 19-C-PMT projection of the same HG-DREAM-simulated pion,
not a separate simulation of the original detector.  Its approximately
hexagonal towers use the published 80 mm apex-to-apex dimension and contain
270 rods each; photons outside the 19-tower footprint are not assigned to an
edge PMT.  All event-display panels use a common logarithmic scale normalized
to the incident explicit-photon count, with zero-occupancy channels left white.

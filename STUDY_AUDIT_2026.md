# FLASH/HG-DREAM dSiPM study audit — October 2026

## Executive conclusion

The scientific direction is sound: HG-DREAM provides realistic local photon
density, a 2 m timing lever arm, and an eventual beam-data validation path for
FLASH. The earlier software is reproducible, but its headline pitch result was
not a physical sensor requirement. A 3 mm segmentation was evaluated on a
1 mm fiber-local display coordinate, so a labelled pitch acted as a cell three
times finer in the DREAMSim coordinates.

The correct conclusion is therefore a requirement surface:

```text
usable photons = incident photons
               x photon-detection efficiency
               x recovery survival(pitch, recovery, shower density)
               x readout survival(grouping, buffers, output policy)
```

No universal 25 um FLASH working point follows from the present samples.

## What remains valid

1. **Historical software closure.** Reproduced ROOT histogram names, binning,
   and integrals agree with the saved electron reference. An independent 40 ns
   recovery implementation matches the historical one-hit calculation point
   by point.
2. **Occupancy mechanism.** Repeated hits in the same cell cause a real local
   dynamic-range loss, strongest in bright hadronic-shower events and near the
   prompt peak.
3. **PDE and recovery separation.** `accepted / incident` and
   `accepted / detected` answer different questions and must both be reported.
4. **Timing motivation.** Photon arrival time is correlated with production
   depth through optical transport, although reflected paths make the mapping
   non-Gaussian and non-invertible photon by photon.
5. **HG-DREAM benchmark choice.** The geometry and beam program provide a
   plausible ladder from optical simulation to present waveform readout and
   later FLASH sparse-hit readout.

## What is superseded

1. **The physical meaning of the historical pitch axis.** The labelled pitch
   behaves as `pitch / 3` in physical DREAMSim coordinates.
2. **A universal 25 um working point.** The old 25 um conclusion was based on
   the display-scaled mapping and cannot specify a fabricated sensor.
3. **Competitor markers on the old curve.** Physical pitches from Litavis,
   ASPIDES, DESY, DIGILOG, DPC3200, or Sherbrooke cannot be projected onto the
   historical curve as performance predictions.
4. **Electron-versus-pion language.** The available large files are 100 GeV
   positrons and 1 TeV pions. They are occupancy stress samples, not a
   controlled species comparison.
5. **Timing-bin arguments by themselves.** A TDC bin, sensor response, timing
   group, and final physics resolution are different quantities.

## Corrected physical study

The current production is `results/flash-study-v2-final/` and is generated by
`studyFlashV2.py`. It uses:

- physical millimeter coordinates with no factor-of-three rescaling;
- a centered 3 x 3 mm sensor;
- one Cherenkov production fiber and a 0--40 ns gate;
- pitches of 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 75, and 100 um;
- constant PDE values of 10%, 20%, 30%, 50%, and 100%;
- non-paralyzable recovery times of 1, 4.3, and 40 ns;
- deterministic common random numbers;
- 400 event-bootstrap replicas for aggregate scan intervals.

Every selected photon falls inside the centered 3 mm sensor. The coordinate

fiber-face image rather than a 3 mm-wide uniform illumination.

### Central numerical comparison

At unity PDE, 25 um pitch, and 4.3 ns recovery:

| Sample | Historical display-scaled survival | Correct physical survival |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100 GeV positron | 98.73% | 90.51% |
| 1 TeV pion | 93.24% | 65.18% |

At 20% PDE and 4.3 ns recovery in the pion stress sample:

| Physical pitch | Usable / incident | Accepted / detected | 10th-percentile event survival |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 um | 19.64% | 97.93% | 96.7% |
| 20 um | 18.59% | 92.73% | 90.3% |
| 25 um | 17.95% | 89.50% | 86.2% |

The 10--20 um range is worth studying under this particular 20% PDE and
4.3 ns assumption. The result is not portable to another PDE, recovery model,
fiber coupling, or shower-energy distribution without rerunning the surface.

### Pulse-shape result

Recovery loss concentrates near the prompt peak. At fixed 20% PDE and 4.3 ns
recovery, the time-dependent accepted/detected ratio falls more strongly for
20 and 25 um pitch than for 10 um pitch. A sensor can therefore preserve most
photons while still biasing a leading-edge or early-quantile observable.

### Timing-depth proof of concept

The current proxy uses the 10th percentile of accepted photon arrival time at
20% PDE, 20 um pitch, and 4.3 ns recovery to predict the mean production depth
of selected photons. Even events calibrate a linear model; odd events test it.

| Scenario | Test RMSE | Added RMSE vs ideal |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ideal photon time | 236.46 mm | 0 |
| 10 ps response + 10 ps TDC | 236.54 mm | 0.08 mm |
| 50 ps response + 50 ps TDC | 236.52 mm | 0.06 mm |
| 250 ps response + 50 ps TDC | 238.69 mm | 2.24 mm |
| 1,145 ps response + 37 ps TDC | 248.25 mm | 11.80 mm |

The ideal median absolute error is 59.24 mm, much smaller than the RMSE,
showing that a long residual tail dominates. Sub-100 ps detector smearing is
not the limiting term for this simple many-photon observable. A 10--50 ps
FLASH target must be justified by richer observables, low-photon regimes, or
an independent system requirement.

## Full-detector replacement study

`studyFullDetector.py` extends the physical sensor model from one fiber to the
available full-detector files. The mapping recovers the repeated 4 mm rod
lattice and the four local fiber positions, so every photon receives a
physical rod, fiber, local cell, and arrival time before digitization.

### Input and branch audit

| Quantity | 100 GeV positron | 90 GeV pion |
|---|---:|---:|
| Events | 1,000 | 100 |
| Selected explicit C photons/event | 13,749 | 9,377 |
| Active C fibers/event | 841 | 1,386 |
| Median photons in brightest C fiber | 621 | 145 |
| Correlation with stored `sum3dCC` | 0.73 | 0.90 |

The explicit optical photons in these files are Cherenkov photons. The files
contain scintillation geometry and stored scintillation sums, but no explicit
scintillation-photon population. Consequently, the explicit-photon and stored
HG-DREAM branches are treated as separate baselines rather than combined into
an apparent sensor bias.

### First detector-level result

At 20% PDE and 4.3 ns recovery:

| Physical pitch | Pion C shift after electron calibration |
|---:|---:|
| 5 um | +0.13% |
| 10 um | +0.47% |
| 20 um | +1.67% |
| 25 um | +2.47% |
| 30 um | +3.28% |
| 50 um | +7.01% |
| 75 um | +12.01% |
| 100 um | +17.59% |

At the 20 um point, the recovery survival is 97.79% for positrons and 99.43%
for pions. The electron-calibrated pion Cherenkov RMS/mean changes from 20.25%
for the matched PDE-only baseline to 19.85% after recovery. That narrowing is
caused by nonlinear compression and must not be presented as improved energy
resolution. The physically useful requirement is particle-dependent response
transfer, not average retained photons alone.

### Conventional-SiPM benchmark

The same full-detector photons are also passed through a representative
Onsemi MicroFC-30035 C-Series analog-SiPM proxy. The data-sheet inputs are 31%
PDE at 420 nm and 2.5 V overvoltage, 4,774 microcells, an 82 ns recharge time
constant, and a 1.5 ns fast-output FWHM. The charge model assigns one
photoelectron to a fully charged avalanche and
`1 - exp(-delta_t / 82 ns)` to a repeated avalanche in the same microcell.

| Readout point | Matched PDE | Electron signal retained | Pion signal retained | Pion C shift after electron calibration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conventional C-Series proxy | 31% | 88.54% | 96.93% | +9.47% |
| FLASH 20 um, 4.3 ns | 20% | 97.79% | 99.43% | +1.67% |

Each response shift is measured against a no-recovery baseline at the same
PDE, so the comparison isolates recovery-driven compression rather than the
different absolute PDE. It shows why a conventional SiPM cannot be represented
by PDE alone in a bright fiber calorimeter: the dense electron core loses more
charge than the pion sample. The model is deliberately optimistic and is not
yet a prediction of the complete present HG-DREAM chain. It omits
wavelength-dependent PDE, recharge-dependent trigger probability, crosstalk,
afterpulsing, electronics noise, and waveform integration. The installed
sensor and front-end parameters must replace this proxy before publication.

The transverse event display is stable at this working point and the losses
are localized in the electromagnetic shower core. This supports the basic
FLASH imaging concept while identifying dynamic range as a local, rather than
detector-average, constraint.

An illustrative fixed-$\chi$ propagation shows that percent-level C
nonlinearity can produce a larger dual-readout bias. It is not yet a final
resolution prediction because the current C and S branches are not generated
from the same explicit photons. The next production must close that loop.

## Current-device interpretation

- Litavis demonstrates 10.17 um physical SPAD pitch, 4 x 4 grouping, 4.3 ns
  holdoff, and a 37 ps TDC bin. Its reported 450 nm instrument-response FWHM is
  1,145 ps, so TDC binning and blue-light timing response must remain separate.
- ASPIDES is close to a calorimeter-oriented counter/first-last architecture,
  but public design values do not yet provide a complete measured response
  model.
- DESY demonstrates digital timing and position readout with timing shared
  within a larger pixel.
- The public blue-light PDE information is insufficient to make an absolute
  HG-DREAM yield prediction for several devices. Proxy curves must be labelled
  as proxies.

## Student-work assessment

The current student repository improves on the capstone workflow by using
separate Geant4 training and prediction batches and a broader energy grid.
Useful pieces have been moved into the tested local response package. The
remaining limitations are:

- QE thinning is unseeded in the original student code;
- recovery is modeled as one hit per event rather than finite holdoff;
- the shift-and-shrink geometry is deliberately distorted;
- the simulation covers one rod with four Cherenkov fibers;
- the wavelength polynomial is a proxy, not a measured device PDE;
- count-only, ideal-sensor, and timing-only baselines are still needed;
- systematic and cross-validation uncertainties need a consistent definition.

The neural-network work should resume after the matched-energy samples and
sensor/output baselines exist.

## Recommended program

1. Produce matched full-detector Cherenkov and scintillation photon samples in
   one configuration, then digitize the present and FLASH readouts from the
   same photons.
2. Produce matched-energy electron, pion, and muon samples at 10, 30, 100, and
   300 GeV with at least 5,000 events per point and complete provenance.
3. Insert measured PDE, instrument-response, and recovery distributions from
   the FLASH prototype and reference devices.
4. Compare all hits, first/last, first per timing group, short histograms, and
   count-only output using the same physics targets.
5. Report shower-depth and PID performance versus bits/event, dropped-hit
   probability, and power proxies.
6. Close optical pulse shape and charge against HG-DREAM beam-test waveforms.
7. Add dark counts, crosstalk, afterpulsing, masking, TDC nonlinearity, buffers,
   and arbitration after the signal-only closure is stable.
